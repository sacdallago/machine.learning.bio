PostDoc opportunity in AI for protein science

We host Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellows and support applications to the MSCA PF call.

This posting is kept for reference. It was written for the 2025 call, whose deadline (10 September 2025) has passed. We continue to support strong applicants in future calls: write to msca at learning.bio. See also Join.

What we offer

  • Joint mentorship across computational and experimental biology from PIs with academic and industry research experience.
  • Research stays across the Technical University of Munich (DE), Duke University (US), and Helmholtz Munich (DE).
  • Direct collaboration with experimentalists to test computational designs in wet-lab assays.
  • Projects in protein design, representation learning, and synthetic biology informed by practical therapeutic and engineering problems.
  • Connection to local research communities including the Protein Design Stammtisch in Munich and the Triangle Protein Design (TriPoD) seminar series in North Carolina.

What we are looking for

A PhD graduate with a background in computational biology, biophysics, physics, or computer science, and:

  • Experience training deep learning models in PyTorch or a comparable framework.
  • Working familiarity with modern architectures for molecules and sequences, such as transformers, graph neural networks, equivariant networks, and diffusion or flow-matching models.
  • Helpful background: hands-on work with protein language models, structure prediction tools (AlphaFold, ESMFold), or distributed training.

Interested?

Send your CV and a short statement of interest to msca at learning.bio to discuss preparing an MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowship proposal.

For the 2025 call, prospective applicants reached out by 15 July ahead of the 10 September 2025 submission deadline.

About us

Michael Heinzinger

Michael earned his PhD (Dr. rer. nat., summa cum laude) in Bioinformatics from the Technical University of Munich in 2022. His dissertation, How to Speak Protein? — Representation Learning for Protein Prediction, adapted self-supervised language modeling methods to protein sequences, showing that learned representations predict structural and functional properties directly from single sequences, and was named a finalist for the Deutsche Studienpreis. Following his PhD, he extended protein language models to incorporate 3D structural information in multimodal architectures such as ProstT5.

At Sanofi, Michael worked as a Computational Scientist on the Biologics x AI Moonshot (BioAIM) team, developing predictive and generative machine learning methods for biologics research.

Since March 2025, Michael leads a research group at the Institute of Computational Biology at Helmholtz Munich and lectures at the Technical University of Munich.

Christian Dallago

Chris earned his PhD in Informatics (summa cum laude) from the Technical University of Munich in 2023, working on self-supervised language models for protein and nucleotide sequences (SeqVec, ProtTrans, Nucleotide Transformer), open-source embedding tools (bio_embeddings), and standardized evaluation benchmarks for protein engineering (FLIP).

Since 2025, Chris holds a dual appointment as Visiting Assistant Professor at Duke University and Senior Research Scientist and Applied Research Team Lead in Digital Biology at NVIDIA. More details are on the Team page.
