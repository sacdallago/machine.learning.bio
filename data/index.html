Bio — longest

Christian Dallago earned his PhD in Informatics (summa cum laude) from the Technical University of Munich in 2023. His doctoral research focused on self-supervised representation learning for protein and nucleotide sequences (SeqVec, ProtTrans, Nucleotide Transformer), open-source protein analysis tools (bio_embeddings, PredictProtein), and standardized benchmarks and data-splitting protocols for protein fitness prediction (FLIP).

At NVIDIA, he has contributed to computational biology research and open releases spanning GPU-accelerated homology search (MMseqs2-GPU), proteome-scale complex structure prediction with the AlphaFold Database, atomistic generative models for protein and biomolecular design (Proteina, La-Proteina, Proteina-Complexa, RFD4-Proteina), and agentic workflows for functional binder design (BindClaw).

Since 2025, Chris holds a dual appointment as Visiting Assistant Professor in Biostatistics & Bioinformatics and in Cell Biology at Duke University, and as Senior Research Scientist and Applied Research Team Lead in Digital Biology at NVIDIA. At Duke, his group works on machine learning methods for biological sequence modeling, molecular design, and rigorous evaluation; at NVIDIA, he leads applied research in computational biology and molecular engineering. He serves on the Board of Directors of the International Society for Computational Biology (2026–2029) and co-chairs the ISCB Industry Advisory Council.

Google Scholar: scholar.google.com. Full CV: christian.dallago.us/CV.pdf.

Bio — long (110 words)

Christian Dallago is a computational biologist developing machine learning methods to interpret, evaluate, and design biological molecules. He earned his PhD in Informatics from the Technical University of Munich in 2023, where he contributed early self-supervised language models for protein and nucleotide sequences (SeqVec, ProtTrans, Nucleotide Transformer) and standardized benchmarks for protein fitness prediction (FLIP). He is a Visiting Assistant Professor in Biostatistics and Bioinformatics and in Cell Biology at Duke University, and a Senior Research Scientist and Applied Research Team Lead in Digital Biology at NVIDIA. His recent research spans GPU-accelerated homology search, proteome-scale complex structure prediction, atomistic protein and biomolecular generation, and agentic workflows for functional binder design.
