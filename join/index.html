What we are working on

Current research directions in the group:

  • Biomolecular design for difficult targets and chemistries. Extending atomistic generative models beyond standard protein–protein interfaces to flexible peptides, modified residues, glycans, nucleic acids, and small-molecule binding pockets.
  • Mechanism-aware and agentic design workflows. Translating biological objectives such as competitive inhibition, multi-partner recruitment, and conformational state selectivity into executable computational assays that guide iterative design.
  • Understanding when sequence pretraining transfers. Analyzing why simple regularized baselines frequently match or outperform fine-tuned protein language models on realistic FLIP2 splits, and identifying settings where pretraining yields reliable gains.
  • Proteome-scale complex prediction and structural analysis. Scaling search and quaternary structure prediction across cellular and viral proteomes, calibrating interface confidence criteria, and characterizing folds and assemblies that form only in complexes.

Two roles, two doors

Chris is a Senior Research Scientist and Applied Research Team Lead in Digital Biology at NVIDIA, and a Visiting Assistant Professor at Duke University, where he leads this lab.

The Duke lab is independent of NVIDIA. Joining the Duke group does not imply employment at, or access to the resources of, NVIDIA. For NVIDIA roles, see the NVIDIA careers page. Everything below concerns the Duke group.

Projects for Duke students

The lab offers supervision and co-supervision for students at Duke. Applicants should be enrolled in the Department of Biostatistics & Bioinformatics or the Department of Cell Biology; co-supervision with faculty in other Duke departments is considered case by case. Currently, we offer co-supervision of PhD students and supervision of MSc and undergraduate students.

To inquire about a project, email lab at learning.bio with:

  1. A concise, one-page CV.
  2. A research project proposal aligned with the lab's themes (250 ± 25 words).
  3. A summary of a recent open-source contribution, with links to code or commits (up to 250 words).
  4. Optional: an academic transcript.

We weigh the open-source contribution heavily when reviewing applications.

Projects for non-Duke students, including international MSc and BSc theses

The lab cannot currently fund external positions or sponsor U.S. visas, so external collaborations take one of two forms:

  • Students with external fellowship funding, or pursuing credit-bearing thesis projects at their home institution, can apply by sending the materials listed above to the same address.
  • We co-supervise internships and theses with partner laboratories in the U.K., Germany, Spain, and South Korea, including groups listed on the Team page.

What to expect

Code, model weights, and datasets from our projects are released openly, and lab members write software intended for external researchers to run. Many projects involve experimental collaborators, with timelines shaped by wet-lab validation rounds alongside computational milestones.

We expect direct, critical discussion of negative and inconclusive results across all projects.

On replies

A note from Chris on email responses:

On a regular work day, I receive many more requests through various communication channels directed to me (as in email "to", not CC, not spam) than I could ever possibly tackle in a day, usually asking for some of my time in the immediate future with some level of urgency. My work days could consist entirely in reacting to incoming requests, but this would result in nothing else getting done, which is not a great long-term strategy, and which usually comes at a cost to all ongoing work streams.

I strive to find a balance of replying and addressing requests, and getting work done, which unfortunately means that I won't always reply to all incoming requests, or sometimes be metered in my replies. Please, don't take the lack of a reply personally, or the brevity to heart!

If you are unsure about an answer, consider setting up a call. If on the other hand you have not received a reply after two working days from your request and still need one, send a ping. A sufficient ping is a reply to your original request with "bringing this up to the top of your inbox again".

A few patterns have emerged in what passes the activation (reply) barrier: be direct, be concise, and be personal. If you need something, summarise it in one sentence or a few bullet points at the top of your email — don't be afraid to be direct!

Thank you for understanding,
Chris.
