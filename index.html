We build machine learning to model, design, and search biology.

The Dallago lab at Duke University.

What we do

We study what machine-learned representations capture about biology, where they break down, and how to turn them into reliable tools for discovery and molecular engineering.

Our research covers self-supervised sequence and structure models, atomistic generative models and agentic workflows for biomolecular design, GPU-accelerated search and complex prediction across proteomes, and benchmarks evaluated under realistic experimental splits.

Research

Representation learning for biology

Self-supervised models for protein and nucleotide sequences.

Generative and programmable design

Atomistic generative models for proteins, binders, and multi-component biomolecules.

Agentic design workflows

Automated systems that translate biological mechanisms into computational assays and iterative design campaigns.

Search and structure at scale

GPU-accelerated homology search and quaternary structure prediction across cellular and viral proteomes.

Benchmarks and evaluation

Datasets and splits that measure generalization in real protein-engineering campaigns.

Selected work

Preprint 2026 · NVIDIA + Duke

Biomolecular design through latent reasoning with RFD4-Proteina

A generative model operating in a shared chemical and conformational latent space across standard and modified amino acids, glycans, DNA, and RNA. It combines recurrent latent refinement with constraint-guided search, with experimental validation across therapeutic binders, post-translational modification recognition, glycosylation control, and de novo enzymes.

Preprint 2026 · NVIDIA

BindClaw: Adapting biological mechanisms for agentic protein design

An agent-assisted workflow that converts a biological objective or paper into a mechanism-specific computational assay and coordinates iterative binder design across multiple generators. In wet-lab testing, 15 of 18 designs bound calcium-loaded calmodulin with subnanomolar to low-nanomolar BLI affinities, and 13 of the 15 showed no detectable binding under calcium-depleted conditions.

Preprint 2026 · NVIDIA + Duke

Systematic exploration of predicted quaternary structures within pandemic-relevant viral proteomes

Monomer and dimer predictions across 2,812 viral proteomes from 23 viral families relevant to human health. Structural clustering consolidated 8,028 high-confidence dimers into 1,598 interface clusters, 29.5% without detectable similarity to experimental interfaces in the PDB, informing glycoprotein vaccinology and viral protease specificity.

Preprint 2026 · NVIDIA + Duke

AlphaFold Database expands to proteome-scale quaternary structures

We predicted 31 million candidate protein complexes across 4,777 proteomes, calibrated confidence criteria against experimentally determined assemblies, and released 1.81 million high-confidence predictions through the AlphaFold Database, 31.3% of which extend beyond detectable PDB coverage.

Nature Methods 2025 · NVIDIA

GPU-accelerated homology search with MMseqs2

GPU-optimized gapless prefiltering and gapped profile alignment for protein sequence search, reaching roughly 100 trillion cell updates per second while maintaining high throughput on consumer and low-wattage GPUs.

ICML 2026 Oral · NVIDIA + Duke

FLIP2

Seven curated fitness datasets and splits covering enzymes, protein–protein interactions, and light-sensitive proteins under realistic distribution shifts, where simple regularized baselines frequently match or outperform fine-tuned protein language models.
